Getting started

How to start

Everything between a new adapter and live data from your car.

  1. Get a compatible adapter

    Voltage reaches your car through a Bluetooth OBD2 adapter — your phone can't talk to the car without one. If you don't have one yet, get the vGate vLinker MC+: it's the adapter we recommend, and it unlocks every feature including remote control. Compare all supported adapters

  2. Plug it into the OBD2 port

    The port sits under the dashboard on the driver's side, usually just above the pedals. Push the adapter in firmly until its indicator lights come on.

  3. Start the car

    Turn the car fully on, so it reads Ready — not just accessory mode. With the car off, most control modules are asleep and can't answer the app.

  4. Connect your phone

    The vLinker MC+ works on both platforms, but each one reaches it a different way.

    1. Pair it in Android's Bluetooth settings first. Open Settings → Bluetooth, and pair the adapter before opening Voltage. If it asks for a PIN, it is usually 1234 or 0000.

    2. Pick the Android device. The vLinker MC+ advertises two separate devices — choose vLinker MC-Android, not the iOS one.

    3. Open Voltage, select that adapter from the list, and press Connect.

    1. Don't pair anything in iOS Settings. On iPhone the adapter is used over Bluetooth LE, and BLE devices never appear in the system Bluetooth list. If you go looking for it there, you won't find it — that's normal.

    2. Open Voltage and switch to the BLE tab in the adapter list.

    3. Pick the iOS device. The vLinker MC+ advertises two separate devices — choose vLinker MC-iOS — then press Connect.

    Once connected, the app reads your VIN and you're ready to scan.

Not connecting?

  • Close any other OBD2 apps. Only one app can hold the adapter at a time, and a forgotten app in the background will keep the connection to itself.
  • Check you picked the right device. On Android make sure it's the -Android entry, on iPhone the -iOS one on the BLE tab. Other adapters often appear under generic names like OBDII or OBDLink rather than a brand name.
  • Turn Bluetooth off and back on. This clears a stale connection that can leave the adapter busy, and fixes most cases on its own.
  • Make sure the car is on, not in accessory mode — that you're within Bluetooth range, and that the adapter's lights are lit.
  • Unpair it from any other phone. Most adapters only accept one paired device, so an old phone in range can quietly take the connection.
  • Still stuck? Unplug the adapter from the OBD2 port, plug it back in, then fully close and reopen the app.

Beware of cheap clones advertising themselves as "ELM327 v2.1" — they're known to answer with scrambled data and can be read as the wrong car. Sticking to a recommended adapter avoids this entirely.
